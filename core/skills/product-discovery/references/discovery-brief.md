# Discovery brief

## Questions to ask (pick what the situation needs)
- **Problem:** Who is affected? What are they trying to do? What happens today (workaround, cost, frequency)? What triggered this request now?
- **Users:** primary and secondary users, roles, contexts of use, accessibility and language needs, permission differences.
- **Outcome:** What changes for the user and for the business if this succeeds? What would make us stop or change course?
- **Scope:** must-have vs nice-to-have; what is explicitly out of scope (non-goals); what existing behavior must not change.
- **Constraints:** deadline and why, budget, team skills, tech stack and integrations, legal/compliance/privacy, platforms, data availability.
- **Dependencies:** other teams, third-party services, migrations, approvals.
- **Risks:** adoption, technical unknowns, data quality, security/privacy, operational load, reputational.

## Assumptions log
| # | Assumption | Type (user / market / technical / legal) | Evidence | How to test | Owner | Status |
Prioritize assumptions by impact if wrong and uncertainty; test the riskiest cheaply first (a question, a prototype, a spike, a manual process).

## Brief template
1. **Problem statement** (one paragraph, no solution).
2. **Users and context.**
3. **Current behavior and pain** (evidence-labelled).
4. **Goals and success metrics** (metric, baseline, target, date, guardrail).
5. **Scope and non-goals.**
6. **Options considered** (at least "do nothing / manual" and one lightweight option, with trade-offs).
7. **Assumptions and risks** (from the log).
8. **Open questions** (who answers, by when).
9. **Recommendation and next step** (what to build or test first and why).
Keep to one or two pages; link detail rather than pasting it.
